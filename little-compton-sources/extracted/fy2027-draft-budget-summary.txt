Summary of FY27 Budget 
We hereby present o ur recommended FY 27 Budget (Draft No. 2 attached ) to the Town Council for 
your  approval for  submission to the Town’s Budget Committee.  We summarize the details of this 
proposal below.  
The FY27 Budget as presented is a balanced budget  whereby revenues are estimated to equal 
budgeted expenditures  at the end of the fiscal year .  
Revenues increased over the FY26 Budget by  $985,421, a 5.55% increase.  Exp enditures excluding 
capital increased over the FY26 Budget by $ 685,421, a 3.86% increase. If we include our proposed 
capital expenditures  of $300,000 in the budget, the total increase  in expenditures  are $985,421,  a 
5.55% increase over  the FY26 Budget.

Expenditure  details  
Personnel  expenses  
Two of the major drivers of the increase in expenditures are salaries and health insurance for Town workers. In this proposal, all required salary increases have been included, according to the three Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBA) in place for FY27.  All other salaries not covered by 
a CBA have been increased by 3.0%.  Additionally, the FY27 Budget reduces one position in the 
Town Clerk’s Office and one position proposed by DPW.  The  Police Department had originally 
proposed two new patrolman and tw o new recruit positions for FY27.  Each of these positions has  
been reduced to only one new position in each  based on discussions with Chief Raynes and the 
ability to be able to hire for these positions.  
Employee insurance  
Based on discussions with the RI Interlocal Trust, our health and dental premiums are estimated to increase by 10.0% and 5.0%, respectively.  These assumptions are all  built into this proposal.  We 
do not anticipate receiving our actual premium increases until late March or early April.  
Pension expense  
The Actuary Report for July 1, 2025, is projecting a pension expense for FY27 of approximately 
$337,000 as compared to the FY26 pension expense of $524,000.  In addition, approximately 
$65,000 of the FY27 pension expense is for School employees; accordingly, this amount will be 
charged to the School Department and will not be included in the Town’s operating budget for 
FY 27.

Debt Service   
The debt service requirements for the School Bond increased by $193,000 over the FY26 Budget.  
This was the result of a favorable reduction in principal debt payment for FY26 resulting from the 
refinancing of the School Bonds during the third quarter of FY 25.  The FY27 debt service has 
increased to levels more in line with years prior to FY26.  
Departmental d etails:  
General Government  
General Government expenses are estimated to  increase  over the FY26 Budget by approximately 
$96,000.  There are cost increases in all categories to cover increases in wages and related payroll taxes.   
A contribution of $25,000 is being proposed for the LC Housing Trust.  This contribution is in 
addition to that of  40% of the Building Permit revenue due to the LC Housing Trust.  
There is an increase in the Town Administrator’s sala ry line item  of approximately $50,000 to fund  
an appropriate salary and fringe be nefits package to attract a  replacement for the current interim 
Town Administrator.   
The Town Clerk proposed hiring an additional person as clerk , to backfill for promotions. T hat 
would give them three people in that office.  We recommend that  we defer hiring for the Clerk’s 
position in that office , given budget constraints and given that the finance and tax offices operate 
with one  staff each .  The overall savings associated with the third position in the Clerk’s Office , 
including fringe benefits,  are approximately $80,000. 
Information Technology  
 
During FY26, the IT Department went from being outsourced to having a full -time IT Director .  
The associated  added costs were covered  by reductions in the Police budget and  Finance 
Department  budget s.  The increases for FY 27 are  the necessary investments needed to maintain an 
effective technology department. Cyber security is a major concern  for all businesses and 
municipalities are becoming a target.  We needed to shore up our systems and provide additional 
controls to protect the assets of the Town.  In addition, an aging infrastructure needs to be 
updated to allow for improved technology.  
 
School – Town Appropriation  
 
The preliminary FY27 Budget reviewed with the School Committee at last week’s School Budget Workshop is calling for a 3.0% increase over the prior year  Town Appropriation.  This represents  
an increase of $247,834.  Under current State law, the School  Committee may not propose a budget 
that requires more than a 4 .0% increase over the preceding year’s Town Appropriation.  In 
addition, the Town has historically funded the pension expense for School employees on the

Police  
The Police reduced their original request by approximately $143,000 by deferring the hiring of one patrolman  and another recruit in FY27.  Deferring  these two hires they  will operate with  10 
personnel instead of 12 which had been agreed upon in the past.  
Dispatch  
Dispatch is showing a  small decrease in their overall budget of approximately  $30,000.  We 
consider ed outsourcing overnight dispatch  services and explored that option with the Chiefs ; 
however, IT and personnel obstacles made it difficult to accomplish for FY27.  
 
Fire 
 
Fire increases by approximately $70,000, which represents an increase of approximately 4.0%.  
Due to minimum manning requirements, it is difficult to cover shifts without adding overtime.  
Accordingly, any absences f or sick , IOD , vacation, etc. will require the use of overtime to fund 
these absences.  
 
Department of Public Works (DPW)  
The DPW includes wastewater, transfer station, highway department, DPW , Tree Warden, 
streetlights , Town Landing and IOOF 32 Commons.  There is a projected increase in the FY27 
Budget as compared to the FY26 Budget of approximately $20,000.  There were some major costs 
for wastewater in FY25 and there will be some ongoing costs necessary to maintain  this system.  In 
addition, a new hire was proposed by the DPW Director ; however, it was determined that the 
current budget would not be able to sustain any new positions in this area.  
Included in the Transfer Station FY27 Budget is $35,000 for  maintenance/improvements .  We 
recommend increasing the price of dump stickers to help offset these costs . A one-time surcharge 
to minimize the impact of such costs  is another option . 
 
OPEB  (Other Post Employee Benefits)  
 
OPEB consists of two types of payments .  The first and the costliest  of the two is the payment of 
health and dental premiums for retired public safety employees  until they qualify for Medicare.  I 
We have been reviewing these payments and I have been able to remove certain former 
employees and their dependents  who no longer qualify for this benefit.

The accompanying budget includes  two additional contracts for potential retirees which has 
added approximately $50,000 to this annual cost. These costs also increased by 10% for health 
premiums and 5% for dental premiums , consistent with the increase for active employees . 
 
The second type of payment is for  two agreements with former employees to pay approximately

Financ e 
 
There is a de crease in this budget for FY27 as compared to FY26 of approximately $67,000.  T here 
are some new  costs attr ibutable to the sharing of a Human Resources Director with the School.  
The School currently employs a n experienced and HR -educated  HR Director who  can add more 
value to the Town by providing her expertise town -wide.  The current HR Director for the School 
not only handles HR at the School but also serves as a n administrative assistant to the 
Superintendent a nd as a payroll clerk.  The intention is to have our current payroll  clerk for the 
Town also input the School payroll.  This will add approximately $20,000 to our current cost .  We 
would reimburse the School a flat $25,000 per year for HR services provided by the School.  In 
essence , the HR function would serve all departments of the Town including the School.  
 
The Finance Director  intend s to stay on through  FY27 year which will allow him  to catch up  on 
some past issues but also put the financial operations back on track, creating the foundation for a 
smooth transition to his replacement.  The Finance Director   salary is set at  $75,000 per year. 
 
The audit fee has been increased to $50,000 and there is also another added $25,000 on the School’s 
FY27 Budget.  We do not yet have a proposal  for the FY26 audit which will be included in the 
FY27 Budget,  but we should have something shortly.  This number does represent a 50% increase 
in fees  which I believe to be reasonable.  
 The Tax Assessor is proposing to promote her current clerk to a Deputy position  and that has been 
provided for in the FY27 Budget.   
 
Public Welfare  
 
This budget consists of the salary for the Director plus the cost of a senior activity coordinator.  In 
addition to the budget of $73,700, there is a grant for appr oximately $9,500 which can be used to 
help support the services provided.  This revenue and any offsetting expenses are not included in 
the FY27 Budget.  
 
Parks and Recreation  
 
Parks and Recreation consist  of maintenance of fields and courts , fireworks, community day, etc. 
as well as support for Wilbour Woods. There were no significant changes to the FY27 Budget as 
compared to the FY26 Budget.

Grants  and Charitable  
 
This amount has decreased from the FY26 Budget by approximately $15,000. 
 
 
Debt Service  
 
Debt service increased in FY27 because of  the refinancing of the School Bond in FY25.  The 
refinancing which saved the Town over $200K resulted in a one -time reduction in principal 
payments in FY26 .  FY 27 debt service for the School Bonds returned to previous levels.  
 
Capital:  
This budget proposal includes capital expenditures of $300,000.  In FY26, all capital expenditures 
were removed from the budget to minimize the needed tax increase to fund the structural deficit.  
In past years, the budget would contain approximately $250, 000 per year which would be set 
aside in the Capital Fund for any capital improvements.  
 
General Operating Capital  
 
The proposed capital expenditures are outlined in the attached budget and are projected to be 
approximately $450,000. To  maintain a balanced budget only $300,000 could be charged to the 
FY27 Operating Budget leaving $150,000 unfunded.  The Town Capital Fund (Fund  420) has a 
current fund balance of approximately  $682,000.  Included in this fund balance are two amounts 
appropriated at financial town meetings of $250,000 and $175,000 for pickleball court 
improvements .  We have been informed by the Town Solicit or that if we want  to use any portion 
of these funds, a vote at a future Financial Town Meeting must take place to reallocate their usage 
for general Town capital.   
The Town Council has already committed approximately $200,000 to $225,000 of the  reserved 
capital funds for fire code improvements at the Community Center , which would leave  only 
$32,000 of the $682,000 available for Town  capital projects.  The major project included in the FY27 
Capital Budget is $275,000 for paving projects approved by the DOT and subject to  a
